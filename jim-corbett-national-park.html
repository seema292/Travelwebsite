About the Reserve

Uttarakhand's Crown Jewel —
Where Project Tiger Was Born

Established 1936 India's 1st National Park
1,318.5 km² Pristine Himalayan Forest
260+ Tigers Highest Density Worldwide

Jim Corbett National Park, established in 1936 as Hailey National Park, holds the legendary honour of being India's first national park and the historic birthplace of Project Tiger in 1973. Situated in the picturesque Nainital and Pauri Garhwal districts of Uttarakhand, it preserves one of the planet's most magnificent ecosystems.

Named in tribute to the celebrated British-Indian conservationist, author, and naturalist Jim Corbett, this protected biosphere is celebrated worldwide for hosting the highest density of Royal Bengal Tigers anywhere on Earth (~260+ resident tigers).

Characterized by towering sal and sheesham woodlands, rolling alluvial grasslands (chaurs), deep ravine riverbeds, and the turquoise waters of the Ramganga River, Corbett provides sanctuary to over 50 mammal species, 25 reptile species, and more than 600 species of resident and migratory birds.

Best Time to Visit

When to Visit Jim Corbett National Park

Every season transforms Corbett's Himalayan landscape. Choose summer for highest predator visibility or winter for crisp misty mornings, elephant herds, and migratory birds.

Best for Tiger Sightings
Summer Season
March – June
As waterholes dry up, tigers, elephants, and leopards congregate along the perennial Ramganga riverbed and reservoir shores. Deciduous forest thins dramatically for peak photography visibility.
Best for Families & Birds
Winter Season
October – February
Crisp misty mornings, sparkling sunny afternoons (10°C–22°C), and snow-capped Himalayan glimpses. Hundreds of migratory waterfowl and raptors arrive across the Ramganga reservoir.
Eco Buffer Drives Open
Monsoon Season
July – September
Core zones (Dhikala & Bijrani) close for wildlife breeding. Jhirna, Dhela, and Sitabani buffer zones remain open for lush, serene eco-safaris with roaring waterfalls and fresh emerald greenery.
March–June: Peak tiger tracking October–February: Family comfort & birds July–September: Eco-buffer year-round

Official forest permits for Jim Corbett can be reserved up to 90 days in advance for Indian nationals and foreign visitors. High-demand zones like Dhikala and Bijrani have strictly limited Gypsy quotas per shift and typically exhaust weeks ahead, particularly for weekends and holidays. Booking 30 to 60 days ahead is strongly recommended.
Dhikala and Bijrani are world-renowned for the highest frequency of Royal Bengal Tiger sightings due to extensive water availability along the Ramganga river and vast grasslands. Jhirna and Dhela zones are also exceptionally productive and have the unique advantage of staying open throughout the entire year.
A 4x4 Gypsy Safari is a private open vehicle accommodating up to 6 guests, offering complete flexibility, privacy, and access to all zones except Dhikala day drives. A Canter Safari is a 16-seater high-clearance open bus that operates exclusively in the Dhikala zone for travelers on day trips without forest rest house night stay permits.
Under Uttarakhand Forest Department regulations, day visitors can access Dhikala zone exclusively via the official 16-seater Canter Safari. Private Gypsy safaris inside Dhikala are strictly reserved for guests with confirmed night-stay bookings inside the historic Dhikala Forest Rest House (FRH).
Indian citizens must provide their Aadhaar Card, Voter ID, or Passport. Foreign nationals must provide their valid Passport and Indian Visa details. The original ID submitted during booking must be carried physically on the safari day, as forest guards verify every traveler at the gate checkpost.
